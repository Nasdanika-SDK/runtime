== From walk-throughs to documents and videos

A recorded walk-through holds what a document about a scenario needs: the steps in order, named; what each looked like; what each asked of the server; what went wrong, where.
With narration (<<narration>>), it also holds what a person would _say_ about each step, in order with the screenshots.
The test that drives the browser writes the script of a video and the text of a guide as it goes, next to the code that does what the text describes, and both are checked by the same run.
Screen recording is built (<<video>>); everything else in this chapter is [.planned]#PLANNED#.

=== Videos

Screenshots and narration records, in the order they were recorded, are a storyboard: a frame per screenshot, held for as long as its narration takes to speak.

* *Narrated videos*: the narration spoken by a text-to-speech service, the screenshots as frames held for the length of their lines, transitions between steps, and subtitles from the same text, rendered with a tool such as FFmpeg. A walk-through recorded by a nightly build is a demo video of last night's application.
* *A video model*: the storyboard as a model of its own, generated from the trace, which a person can reorder, trim and annotate before rendering, and which is regenerated, keeping their changes, when the walk-through changes.
* *Narrated screen recordings*: a session's video is recorded already, with each step's offset into it (<<video>>). Laying the narration over the recording at the offsets of the steps that narrate it gives motion instead of stills.

=== Documents

* *User guides in AsciiDoc and Markdown*: a section per step with its narration as the text and its "after" screenshot, generated from the trace and rebuilt with the application, so the pictures and the words are never out of date.
* *Slide decks*: a slide per step, its narration in the speaker notes, through the runtime's presentation and PowerPoint models, for demos and reviews.
* *Sequence diagrams* of a step: the browser, the server and what the server called, from the tree of spans, laid out with the runtime's diagram model and ELK.
* *Process flows* of a scenario: steps as activities, generated as a diagram.
* *Comparisons of runs*: steps added or removed, requests added, slower or failing, screenshots that changed.

The trace is the source of all of them, so each is a transformation of one model into another, and none needs the test to know about it.
One walk-through, one test, becomes a guide, a deck, a diagram and a video, in step with each other and with the application.
