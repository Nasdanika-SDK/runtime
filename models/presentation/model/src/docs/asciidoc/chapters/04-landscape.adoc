[#landscape]
== The landscape

*Libraries of formats*: Apache POI, the Open XML SDK, python-pptx, Aspose.Slides, the ODF Toolkit, PDFBox.
These are the sources, not the rivals: loaders and generators are built on libraries like them, or on models of the formats' schemas.
Each gives a tree of objects shaped like its format: excellent for writing one file, of no use as a place to keep an estate of presentations, and with no notion of the element a shape stands for.

*Services of conversion and office suites without a screen*: CloudConvert, GroupDocs, LibreOffice's `--convert-to`, the exporters of Visio, the import of Visio files into Draw.io.
They are the pragmatic incumbent for interchange.
They are pairwise, one-way and opaque: there is nothing in the middle to inspect, correct, validate, enrich or version, so a conversion that loses shape data loses it silently.
And what they lose first is usually what mattered, Visio shape data, Draw.io properties, notes, because it is invisible in the output.

*Decks as code*: Reveal.js, Marp, Slidev, remark, LaTeX Beamer, Quarto, Pandoc.
They are text first, versioned, and good at producing HTML or PDF from Markdown.
The source is the artifact, with no graph of objects to query and no stable identity of a shape, and the output is terminal: a business audience asks for a deck it can edit.
They make good input formats for a model.

*Diagrams as code*: PlantUML, Mermaid, D2, Graphviz, the Structurizr DSL.
Sources, not competitors.
The interesting direction is the one they cannot take themselves: a PlantUML sequence diagram arriving in a deck as editable shapes and connectors, not as an embedded picture.

*Software as a service and generators of decks with AI*: Canva, Pitch, Gamma, Copilot in PowerPoint, Google Slides with Gemini.
The fastest way to get a deck, and they produce no data: nothing to validate the result against, no comparison of this quarter's deck with the last beyond reading both, and no path from the deck to any other tool.

*Standards.*
The OMG has a Diagram Definition specification and interchange packages per notation; there is nothing for presentations.
The deck, the most widely produced structured visual artifact in the enterprise, has never had a model for interchange.

*The actual competitor*: a deck on a shared drive, a library of Visio drawings that one retiring architect can read, and a slide rebuilt by hand every time the audience changes.
